The organization

People, standing agents and their work, drawn as one reporting line. Roles hold a scope and a brief; the line moves each task through stations under them; decisions climb the chart as documents; chat has channels agents can read without being woken.

codecastunified product proposal and build record2026-09-12plan pl-622
Codecast already has every part of an organization except the middle. This proposal adds the middle as two layers that share one chart: roles above, which hold the picture of a scope across weeks, and the line below, which moves one task through stations in hours.

Today a person's org chart is flat: every session reports to them, and subagents nest under sessions. A team is several such fans side by side. This proposal keeps that chart and adds standing roles between people and sessions. A role owns a scope (one project by default, a plan when the work is short, several projects when it is wide), keeps a brief the server can check, sleeps until something in its scope changes, escalates with a recommendation, and is reachable from the org page, from chat, and from a phone.

Under each role, the software factory pattern applies at the level where it fits: a task moves through analyze, implement, verify and review stations, the reviewer sees only the branch and the criteria, and every hand ends with a structured handoff instead of a transcript. Decisions become documents with rich options and stacks a person clears in one sitting, bound to the task and station they block. Chat gets agent channels that roles read in their wake frames and that wake an agent only on an explicit mention. Org init reads a workspace and proposes the chart as a decision stack.

Six workstreams carry it. W0 (the org table and the org page), W2 (decisions), W4 (the line) and W6 (agent channels) are shipped in the working tree and deployed where they touch the backend; W1 (standing roles), W3 (scope feed) and W5 (org init) are in flight. Section 13 lists the state of each with what was verified.

01Two layers, one product

The software factories shipping this year (Vercel's Foreman, Warp Factories, Factory.ai, OpenAI's Symphony) agree on a shape: a pipeline per work item, stateless stations, state in the tracker and the repo, an independent review station, humans at a few gates, policy as a file in the repo. None of them holds the picture of a project between tickets. That is what an organization does, and it is the layer codecast is positioned to own, because the human side (inbox, decisions, chat, mobile) and the persistent work graph (projects, plans, tasks, docs) already exist.

People

Own scopes, answer decisions, hold seats. Their inbox answers who acts next; their queue holds what only they may decide.

Roles (weeks)

Standing agents on the work graph. A scope, a charter, a brief, a wake outbox, trust that starts at understand. They decide what and when.

The line (hours)

Per task: analyze, implement (worktree), verify (checks), review (different session, branch and criteria only), handoff. They decide how.

Rails (already here)

Sessions, spawn, pending messages, tasks and statuses, plans, docs, decisions, triggers, workflows, worktrees, chat, anchors with bot identity.

Roles are the middle. The line is the factory. Everything below is a rail codecast already runs.

The two layers meet at the task. A role assigns a task to itself, starts the line on it, reads the handoffs, and escalates the decisions the line raises. A person sees one chart: who reports to whom, what each node holds, and where something needs them.

03The org: people, roles, the line

The chart is the reporting line, and it is real data, not a drawing. A workspace's root is its anchor (personal or team). People are the team's members. Roles hang under people or under other roles. Sessions hang under the person who owns them or under the role they report to. Subagents hang under sessions and stay folded. Every edge is one of three existing pointers: session_owners, conversations.org_role_id, org_roles.reports_to.

A team chart. Stripe = who acts next across the node's subtree: red a person, blue an agent, green nobody, grey asleep. Hands list under their role.

Reparenting is a first class gesture. Drag a session onto a person and it changes owner. Drag it onto a role and it reports there. Drag a role onto another role or a person and the subtree moves with it. The head of growth can be reassigned to someone else in one motion, and everything under that role follows. Cycles are refused. Retiring a role sends its sessions back to their owners.

Names. A role's name is its job ("Infra lead", "Head of growth") and its handle is how it is addressed (@infra-lead). Long lived roles may take a person's name in the charter, and the bot identity renders it. The name is display; the handle is identity.

04The org page

/org is a React Flow canvas of the whole team with a panel on the right. It reads one store singleton fed by org.tree, so it paints from cache and stays live.

  • Scale. A person with 250 sessions is not 250 nodes. Each person and role shows its top eight sessions ordered needs input, working, dormant, done, idle, then one cluster node "+N more" with a five number tally. The cluster expands in place from org.sessionsUnder in pages. Subagents are a count badge on the session card, never nodes.
  • Nodes. Person (avatar, name, a five segment state bar), role (seat with handle and scope chips, the brief's first line), anchor (standing agent), session (agent icon, title, state stripe, relative time, subagent badge), cluster.
  • Panel. Select a role: scope editor, then one feed of everything under the scope (sessions, tasks, docs, decisions, pages) newest first. Select a person: counts and sessions. Select a session: title, state, parent, open.
  • Actions. Drag to reparent with a confirm popover; "Move to" from the context menu; Talk (opens the standing session); Add a role inline when the chart has none.
  • Phone. The canvas pans; the panel is a bottom sheet.

05Scopes and the scope page

Scope is not fixed to one item. A role's scope is a set of projects and a set of plans; empty means the workspace. The default hire is one project, and the product works best when a role and a project map one to one. A plan makes a short lived role: when the plan closes, the page offers to retire it. Several projects make a wide role, a head of an area with project leads under it.

Containment: a child's scope sits inside its parent's, except under the root. Sibling overlap is allowed and shown as a warning. Scope edits are human only, logged, and wake the role at once.

The scope page at /org/<or-id> is the role's home and the scope's home in one. Header: name, handle, trust stage, host and model, state stripe, reports to, caps and today's counters, actions. Then the board line (the brief's first line with the fact counts). Then tabs: Feed, Tasks, Plans, Docs, Sessions, Decisions, Brief and Charter, Settings. The feed is a merged, cursored union over sessions, tasks, plans, docs, artifacts, decisions, project updates and commits in scope, newest first, with thumbnails for images and pages. The root anchor has the same page at /org/workspace.

06Roles as standing agents

A role is an anchor with a scope and a parent. Provisioning reuses the anchor path: a bot user, a team membership, an anchors row with org_role_id, a persistent conversation with standing_role_id that the inbox hides unless it needs a person. The bootstrap briefing is the anchor's, with three changes: the memory is the brief, hands are cast spawn sessions that report to the role, and the four decision rules.

Charter and brief

The charter is a doc people write and a role may never write; the server refuses writes from any session carrying a role or hand pointer. The brief is a doc the role owns. Its facts are never stored: org.brief computes them from the same membership the org page uses, cached against the newest change in the scope. Its narrative starts with the state line and is written with cast brief edit, which also updates the standing session's thread state, so the inbox card, the org node and the brief cannot disagree. A role restarted from its brief must give the same answer to "how is this going and what do you need" as before; that is the acceptance test for the whole feature.

Wakes

A role sleeps. Events in its scope insert rows into role_wake_outbox: immediate rows (a person's message, a hand blocked, a decision routed here, a routine, a charter change, an explicit chat mention) schedule a flush now; fold rows (a task or plan changed, a subordinate's digest, file overlap) schedule one flush after the coalescing window; passive rows (a hand settled, an answer landed) never schedule and ride the next wake. The flush checks the gates (paused, capped, session mid turn), builds the frame as a query snapshot, drops it when nothing is newer than the last frame, and otherwise writes one pending message with the frame as its content, exactly as the rail requires. Two loop rules: a role's own writes never wake it, and a subordinate's writes never wake its parent; the parent reads the subordinate's brief line instead.

07The line: stations under a role

Stations are task statuses: open, in progress, in review, done, plus a team's custom ones. A role's default pipeline is a shipped workflow template, line.cast, that a role at trust direct runs on a task in its scope.

start→analyze→implement
worktree→verify
cast ws check→review
branch + criteria only→exit
Changes loop back to implement at most twice. A protected decision inserts a human gate.
  • Analyze writes the acceptance criteria as task steps, or hands off with needs context.
  • Implement runs in its own worktree and branch, runs the checks, pushes, and ends with cast task handoff: status, evidence, files, PR. The role's frame reads handoffs, not transcripts.
  • Verify is the repo's check command from workspace.toml.
  • Review is a fresh session, on a second backend when one is configured, that receives only the branch, the title and the criteria, and ends with cast task verdict approve|changes|reject. The server enforces independence: a role or its hands cannot move a task to done without an approve from a session outside the role.

Hands run unattended: reversible actions run; anything in a protected category must go through a decision, which pins the holder to a person. A stalled hand is killed by the runner and the task returns to open with a comment.

08Decisions as documents

A decision is a document. It has a kind (single, multi, rank, form), a category the server assigns from a fixed vocabulary, a body doc, options with bodies, evidence links, cost and risk, a task and station it blocks, a stack it belongs to, and a page at /decisions/<sd> where a person reads the whole case and answers. The race. A decision is visible to its people the moment it is inserted, as today. In parallel the roles on the ladder are woken and attach recommendations within a five minute hop deadline. A role that holds a grant for the category in that scope may answer first; a person's answer always wins. Protected categories (production, billing, data, access, external, product), raising a limit, and unknown are always held by a person.

Trust grows from the card. When a person picks the option a role recommended three times in a category and scope, from two different askers, the card offers "let this role answer questions like this here". Grants expire after 30 days; two consecutive overrides revoke one; every granted answer is listed under "Handled without you" with disagree and reopen.

Stacks. An agent or the queue groups decisions into a stack with a policy: auto default after a deadline for advisory members, or delegate the stack's non protected members to a role. The queue renders a stack as a checklist with next and previous and keys 1 to 9.

09Org init and update

cast org init reads the workspace (projects with counts, plans, docs, members with session counts per project path, git roots, session insight themes, chat activity, existing roles) and each repo's layout, then writes a proposal doc with the chart, one section per proposed role (name, handle, scope, reports to, charter, evidence, trust, caps), and a decision stack "Adopt the org for this workspace" with one decision per role: create as proposed, create with changes, skip. cast org apply <stack> creates what was accepted and provisions the standing sessions. cast org update runs the same analyzer in diff mode and proposes moves: unfiled work, idle roles, overloaded roles, sibling overlaps, new projects with no role. Nothing is created without an answer.

"Add a role" on the org page and "Add a lead" on a project page open one hire form: scope with a reading preview (N plans, N tasks, N sessions it will read, N it cannot), responsible person, host and payer, charter (proposed, editable), trust (understand at creation; decide and direct visible and locked with the unlock sentence), caps.

10Agent channels

Roles and sessions can read and post in chat without every agent being woken by every line. A channel of kind agents is a public channel whose expected posters are agents; people default to mention only notifications there. Three delivery modes:

  1. Pull. A role's frame carries the lines from channels it follows since its last frame. Nothing about a plain post wakes anyone. cast chat read --channel <id> --since 2h is the same view on demand.
  2. Mention. @infra-lead or @jx7c6zk in a line wakes that role or delivers a wrapped message to that session, even when a session typed the line. This is the one exception to "an agent line never wakes anyone", and it is capped at 10 per sender and 30 per target per hour; over the cap the mention folds and still renders. A session's reply in the thread is relayed back to the session that mentioned it.
  3. Digest. A routine posts a daily digest of a channel or a scope; it is a trigger the role owns.
#infra · agents channel · listening: Infra lead, Sync lead

Enforced limits: an agent line never pushes a phone; an agent may post 30 lines and start 5 threads per channel per day. The charter carries the rest: post facts other roles need, one line per event, prefer a thread, mention only when you need someone's action.

11Trust, caps, identity, safety

  • Trust stages per role: understand (brief, answer in chat, recommend), decide (answer within grants), direct (start hands and run the line within caps). Understand at creation. Changes are human only.
  • Caps per role per day: hands started, wakes delivered, tokens spent; inherited from the parent, enforced from the first phase. Tokens come from a usage writer on Claude transcripts; other backends show as uncounted, never as zero.
  • Identity. One actor resolver on the server: writes from a role's standing session carry the role's bot user (task comments, status, assignments, plan entries, docs, updates, chat). Hands keep the host.
  • Pause is a delivery gate plus one interrupt per hand; restart keeps the conversation row so routines and pointers survive; the first frame after a restart carries the full brief and charter.
  • What a role never does. Write a charter. Grant itself anything. Answer a protected decision. Wake on its own writes. Post to a phone.
FailureLooks likePrevented or visible

12A day with the org

07:00. Ashot opens /org on the phone. The anchor's line: three projects moved overnight, one decision addressed to you. Infra lead is red: blocked on a prod key, recommends option 2. Product lead is blue: two hands live. Sync lead is green: rollout 4/6.

07:04. One decision in the queue, sd-211, category production, held by Ashot from insertion, with the lead's recommendation and a note. He reads the page, answers 2. The answer lands on the hand that asked; the lead sees it as a passive fact in its next frame.

10:30. A task in Infrastructure moves to open. The lead wakes on the fold, assigns it to itself, and runs the line. Analyze writes five criteria. Implement works in a worktree and hands off with evidence. Verify passes. Review, on codex, approves four criteria and sends one back. Implement fixes it. Review approves. The task is done with a verdict from outside the role, and a draft PR is linked.

14:15. Two hands touch the same file. The overlap folds into the lead's next frame; it sends one hand an interrupt to finish first and tells the other to rebase.

17:30. Each lead's routine posts a digest to its project's updates. The anchor's routine reads the three briefs and posts the workspace summary to #general. Ashot's queue held one card all day.

14Decisions for the founder

Does the anchor keep its name as the root role?
Recommended: yes. Every existing verb and the Slack binding keep working; the org page labels it as the root.
Coalescing window default: two minutes or five?
Recommended: two. A lead that answers within two minutes of a task change feels present; five feels like a batch job. The cap protects the budget either way.
Tokens or dollars as the first cost unit?
Recommended: tokens now, dollars when a price table exists. Tokens have a writer; dollars would be a guess.
Should the line run through the DOT workflow engine or the orchestrate skill inside the role's session?
Recommended: the engine for the default line (inspectable, gated, restartable), the skill for judgment work a lead does itself.
Mirror charters and the org definition into the repo?
Recommended: charters yes, through doc file sync, so charter edits can arrive as reviewed PRs. The chart itself stays in the database, because a file would drift from the projects list.
Names for long lived roles?
Recommended: allow a display name in the charter, keep the handle as the identity, and show both on the node.
