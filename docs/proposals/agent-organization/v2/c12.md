## 12. Failure modes and the version one contract

### Product failure modes

Version one enumerated thirty two failure cases at the level of transactions, credentials and recovery. They stand. This chapter adds the failures that only appear once there is a product on top: the ways an organization of agents can be technically correct and still make the human's life worse.

| Failure | What it looks like | Defence in this design |
|---|---|---|
| Brief drift | A brief that is fluent, confident and slowly wrong, because the lead summarised its own previous brief instead of rereading its scope | Cadence wakes reread the scope from records, not from the previous brief. Briefs are diffed. A critic agent samples claims against sources weekly and the health page shows the miss rate. |
| Sycophantic lead | Recommendations that agree with whatever the human seemed to want | Track record counts agreement, but the sampled claim audit is independent of the human. Overrides are recorded with reasons and reviewed on the health page. |
| The human stops reading | The brief is good, so the human trusts it and stops zooming, and a bad claim goes unnoticed | Claimed versus verified is a visual distinction on every line. "Claimed done, review pending" is never rendered like "verified". Coverage is in the footer. |
| Interruption flood | A busy plan wakes its lead continuously, and the lead rewrites its brief so often the workspace lead never sees a stable one | Coalescing with a floor. Brief rewrites carry a watermark, and a parent reads the latest complete version, never a partial. |
| Runaway direction | A trusted lead spawns workers to fix a problem its workers caused, in a loop | Admission against budget bounds it in dollars. Causal depth from version one bounds it in generations. The brief must list every start, so the loop is visible on the next read. |
| Trust creep | Grants accumulate until a lead decides most things and the human has forgotten what it may do | Every grant is on the lead's page with the date and the card it was granted from. Two consecutive overrides revoke. The health page lists grants per lead. |
| Lead disagreement | A plan lead and its project lead recommend opposite things | The ladder resolves it: the project lead's recommendation is the one that reaches the human, with the plan lead's attached. Disagreement between leads is itself a reason to forward. |
| Stale delegation | A grant made when a scope was small still applies after the scope has grown | Grants are per scope and the scope's membership is shown when a decision is answered under a grant. A refiled task carries no grants with it. |
| Silent scope | A lead cannot read half its scope because of audience and does not say so | Coverage is a required field of the brief format. A brief with unknown coverage cannot be saved. |
| Cost surprise | The human learns at the end of the month what the organization cost | Cost with coverage is one of the five sections of every brief, at every level, every day. |

### What this version changes, and why

| Version one decision | This version | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Start from observation of selected tasks | Start from a brief of the whole readable scope | A brief of selected tasks cannot answer "how is the project doing", which is the first thing the human wants. Audience rules already bound what is read. |
| Defer plan leads until a project pilot shows value | Plan leads are optional from the start, and the third dogfood step | The plan is where orchestration already happens in codecast today. A plan lead is the natural home for the existing implementer and reviewer flow. |
| Trust granted through a policy revision the human edits | Trust granted from the decision card after observed agreement | Nobody can predict in advance which classes a lead will be right about. Evidence at the moment of decision is the only honest input. |
| One new table for usage | As many records as the contracts require | Version one's own review already retracted the one table constraint. |
| Brief defined as an optional digest field on a doc | Brief is a first class, fixed shape, versioned document with required coverage | The brief is the product. Its shape is what makes the chain of understanding work. |
| Chat intake as the primary way work enters | Chat intake is one way; the brief and the task board are the primary surfaces | Most work already exists as tasks. Intake handles the new; the brief handles the existing. |

### Glossary

- **Lead.** The role that understands, decides and directs at workspace, project or plan scope. The workspace lead is the existing anchor.
- **Worker.** A session bound to a task. Human or agent.
- **Brief.** A lead's live, versioned, sourced account of its scope, in five fixed parts.
- **Standing brief.** The lead's job description, written by the human.
- **Ladder.** The reporting line a decision climbs: worker, plan lead, project lead, workspace lead, human, skipping missing levels.
- **Grant.** Permission for a lead to answer one decision class in one scope, given from a decision card after observed agreement.
- **Track record.** A lead's agreements, overrides and sampled claim accuracy, on its page.
- **Coverage.** The fraction of a scope a brief could read, stated on every brief.
- **Claimed, verified, accepted.** A worker's completion, a reviewer's check, and the human's acceptance. Three different words on every surface.
