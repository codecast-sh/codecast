## 10. Rollout

The rollout is gated by trust, not by calendar. Each stage unlocks one verb from chapter 2 and each stage has an exit that is measured on this workspace, which is the first customer.

### Exit criteria

Each stage exits on evidence gathered by using the feature on this workspace for at least two working weeks. The criteria are deliberately few and each is something the product itself can measure.

| Stage | Exit when |
|---|---|
| Understand | The human reads the morning brief on most working days by choice, measured by open events. Fewer than one in ten brief claims sampled by a critic agent lack a valid source. Brief coverage above ninety percent of sessions in scope. No brief has claimed a completion that was not verified. |
| Decide | At least three classes granted in at least two scopes. Override rate on granted classes below one in ten over the period. Median time from a worker's ask to an answer, for granted classes, under ten minutes. No protected class answered by a lead, verified by audit. |
| Direct | Ten tasks started by leads reach verified completion. Every start admitted; zero unadmitted processes discovered by the daemon. Stall detection caught every stall a human noticed, checked weekly. Cost coverage above ninety percent priced. Replacement drill passed on a live lead. |

If a stage does not exit, the feature stays at the previous stage for that scope and the reason is written in the health page. There is no calendar date by which a stage must ship.

### Dogfood plan

This workspace has eight projects, one anchor, and a human who reads sixty six sessions. It is the pilot. The order is chosen so that each step is useful on its own.

1. **Workspace lead only.** Give the existing anchor the brief format, the cadence wake and the morning brief page. Nothing else. This already replaces the human's first twenty minutes of triage with reading, and it exercises brief writing on raw task and session state, the hardest case.
2. **One project lead, Codecast.** The busiest project, with plans, a chat channel and a repo. Its brief feeds the workspace brief. This exercises the chain.
3. **Decisions through the ladder.** Route this project's decisions through its lead with recommendations. Watch the agreement rate.
4. **First grants.** Let the Codecast lead answer approach and ordering questions once the card offers it.
5. **One plan lead**, on a plan with more than five tasks, at direct. This is the first lead that spawns work.
6. **Remaining projects**, one at a time, as their owners choose.

### What is measured throughout

- Time the human spends in the inbox per day, from client events, before and after each stage.
- Decisions per day reaching the human, and their class mix.
- Agreement and override rates per lead and class.
- Brief freshness at the moment it is read.
- Cost per scope with coverage.
- Every stall, whether the organization noticed it, and how long before it did.

None of these are targets. They are the instruments the exit criteria read. Version one was right that invented baselines do not belong in a proposal, and this version sets none.

### Compatibility and rollback

Every stage is additive. A workspace with no leads behaves exactly as codecast does today. Retiring every lead returns the workspace to today's behaviour with the briefs, decision logs and track records kept as readable history. Backend changes ship before the clients that call them, through the existing deploy script, and enriching queries on existing surfaces use the no throw hook so a missing function degrades to today's view rather than an error boundary.

### Decisions for the founder

Six choices in this proposal are matters of product taste rather than evidence. They are listed here so they can be decided without rereading the document, and they are queued as decisions from the session that wrote it.

1. **Name.** "Lead" for the role, and "brief" for its account, or a different pair.
2. **Where the morning brief lives.** As the front page for anyone with an anchor, or as a tab beside the inbox.
3. **Trust threshold.** Three agreements to unlock a grant, and two consecutive overrides to revoke it, or different numbers.
4. **Default rhythm for project leads.** Continuous with coalescing, or a fixed cadence.
5. **Independent review by default.** Required for every task closed by a lead, or decided by effect class as proposed.
6. **First pilot project.** Codecast, or a smaller one.
