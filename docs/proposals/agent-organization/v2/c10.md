## 10. Rollout

The rollout is gated by trust, not by calendar. Each stage unlocks one verb from chapter 2 and each stage has an exit that is measured on this workspace, which is the first customer. Delivery of decisions to the human does not change at any stage; stage one adds recommendations, stage two adds grants, stage three adds direction.

### Exit criteria

Each stage exits on evidence gathered by using the feature on this workspace for at least two working weeks. These thresholds are proposed defaults. Version one's rule stands: measure a baseline first and revise them from it.

| Stage | Exit when |
|---|---|
| Understand | Hire to first sourced brief under 10 minutes on the pilot project, and no lead creates a task to show activity. The human reads the morning brief on most working days by choice, measured by open events. Fewer than 1 in 10 brief claims sampled by a critic agent lack a valid source. Brief coverage above 90% of sessions in scope. No brief has rendered a claimed completion as verified. |
| Decide | At least three classes granted in at least two scopes. Override rate on granted answers, read from the sampled cards and the handled controls, below 1 in 10 over the period. No protected class answered by a lead, and no granted answer whose option text names a protected effect, verified by audit. |
| Direct | 10 tasks started by leads reach verified completion. Every start admitted; zero unadmitted processes discovered by the daemon. Stall detection caught every stall a human noticed, checked weekly, with no stall message loop. Cost coverage above 90% priced. Replacement drill passed on a live lead. Mobile stop and take over exercised on a live worker. |

If a stage does not exit, the feature stays at the previous stage for that scope, and the human who ran the stage writes the reason on the health page. There is no calendar date by which a stage must ship.

### Dogfood plan

This workspace has 8 projects, one anchor, and a human who reads 66 sessions. It is the pilot. The order is chosen so that each step is useful on its own.

0. **Baseline.** 7 working days of the instruments below on today's inbox, before any lead exists, so "before and after" has a before.
1. **Workspace lead only.** Give the existing anchor a policy revision with turn and wake caps, the safe mode, the brief format, the cadence wake and the morning brief page. Nothing else. This replaces the human's first pass of triage with reading, and the time saved is the first thing the rollout measures. It exercises brief writing on raw task and session state, the hardest case.
2. **One project lead, Codecast.** The busiest project, with plans, a chat channel and a repo. Its brief feeds the workspace brief. This exercises the chain.
3. **Recommendations on the ladder.** This project's decisions gain recommendations from its lead. Watch the agreement rate.
4. **First grants.** Let the Codecast lead answer approach and ordering questions once the card offers it.
5. **One plan lead**, on a plan with more than five tasks, at direct. This is the first lead that spawns work.
6. **Remaining projects**, one at a time, as their owners choose.

### What is measured throughout

- Time the human spends in the inbox per day, from client events.
- Decisions per day reaching each human, and their class mix.
- Agreement and override rates per lead and class, including overrides on handled items.
- Brief freshness at the moment it is read, and claims dropped per reader.
- Cost per scope, with the lead's own line and coverage.
- Every stall, whether the organization noticed it, and how long before it did.

### Compatibility and rollback

Every stage is additive. A workspace with no leads behaves exactly as codecast does today. Retiring every lead returns the workspace to today's behavior with the briefs and decision records kept as readable history. Deploy order and the fallback for clients ahead of prod are in chapter 11.

### Decisions for the founder

Seven choices in this proposal are matters of product taste rather than evidence. They are listed here so they can be decided without rereading the document, and they are queued as decisions from the session that wrote it.

1. **Name.** "Lead" for the role, and "brief" for its account, or a different pair.
2. **Where the morning brief lives.** As the front page for anyone with an anchor, or as a tab beside the inbox.
3. **Trust threshold.** Three agreements from two askers to unlock a grant, two consecutive overrides to revoke it, 30 day expiry, or different numbers.
4. **Default rhythm for project leads.** Continuous with the 5 minute window, or a fixed cadence.
5. **Independent review by default.** Required for every task a lead closes, as version one proposed, or by effect class with a human override on the plan, as proposed here.
6. **Lead sessions in the inbox.** Stashed and hidden by default, as proposed, or visible like any session.
7. **First pilot project.** Codecast, or a smaller one.
