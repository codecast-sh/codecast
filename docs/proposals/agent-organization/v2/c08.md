## 8. The lead's contract

A lead is an agent session running a standing brief, waking on a rhythm, with the cast CLI as its hands. This chapter is that contract: what a lead is told, when it runs, what it may do, and how it is replaced. It is written at the level of principle. The exact prompt is an implementation detail that will be tuned by measurement, and the house rule for prompts applies: state the intent, and escalate to directive instructions only where the principle fails.

### The standing brief

The standing brief is the lead's job description. The human writes it when they hire the lead, from a proposed default, and edits it whenever they like. It is a document. The lead reads it at the start of every session and whenever it changes. It has four parts and the default text for each is proposed here as principle, not as final wording.

**Who you are.** You are the lead for this scope on behalf of a named person. You coordinate; you do not own. The person owns the outcomes. Your reader is the level above you, who has less context and less time than you.

**What matters.** The human's priorities for this scope in their own words. For a project: what it is for, what good looks like this month, what to leave alone. This part is mostly human authored and the proposed default is a short prompt for the human to fill.

**How you work.** Understand before you decide, decide before you direct. Read compressed signals first, transcripts on suspicion. Write your brief for a reader in a hurry, sources on every claim, coverage stated. Recommend only when you have a view; forward when you do not. Never answer what belongs to the human. Never message a session when reading it would do. Never spend outside your budget. Say what you cannot see.

**What to escalate.** The protected classes, always. Anything the human said matters in "what matters". Anything you are unsure of. A stall you cannot resolve within your trust. A disagreement between leads below you.

The existing anchor already has a standing briefing, sent by `cast anchor brief`, that covers who it is, its routines and how it reaches people. The lead's standing brief is that document generalized to a scope. One mechanism, three scopes.

### Rhythm

A lead runs on wakes. It is dormant between them, in the existing sense: parked on a machine wake, filed under dormant, costing nothing. There are three kinds of wake and all three ride the existing trigger machinery.

| Wake | Cause | What the lead does |
|---|---|---|
| Event | A decision climbs to it; a child scope changes state; a worker finishes, stalls or asks; a chat mention; a task is refiled into its scope; a lead below it rewrites its brief | Handles the event, rewrites the affected part of its brief, goes dormant |
| Cadence | The time the human chose: before 08:30 for a workspace lead, hourly for a busy plan | Rereads its scope, rewrites the whole brief, goes dormant |
| Ask | A human or another lead sends it a message or mentions it | Answers, from brief first and live state second, goes dormant |

Event wakes are coalesced. A plan with five workers finishing in the same minute wakes its lead once with five events, not five times. The coalescing window is a lead setting with a sensible default and a floor, and an ask from a human never waits for the window. Version one specified the durable event inbox that makes coalescing safe; this version uses it.

A lead's turn is short by design. It reads, it writes, it forwards, it stops. A lead that finds itself running a long investigation is doing a worker's job and should spawn a worker instead. The standing brief says so.

### Tools

A lead's hands are the cast CLI, and only the parts of it that a manager needs. The proposed allow list for a lead at stage one, understand, is read commands plus its own documents: `cast sessions`, `cast read`, `cast diff`, `cast task ls`, `cast task show`, `cast plan show`, `cast project show`, `cast chat read`, `cast doc` on its own docs, `cast state`, `cast decide` to forward with a recommendation. Stage two, decide, adds answering decisions in granted classes. Stage three, direct, adds `cast task start --spawn`, `cast spawn --subagent`, `cast send` to sessions in its scope, `cast task update` and `cast task done` on tasks in its scope, and the tidy verbs.

The allow list is enforced by the scoped credential that version one designed, not by the prompt. The prompt tells the lead what it should do; the credential decides what it can. A lead on a host that cannot enforce the credential stays at stage one, and the hiring flow says so before the human invests in the standing brief.

### Writing

A lead writes four things and nothing else.

- **Its brief**, in the fixed five part shape from chapter 3, as a versioned doc.
- **Its state line**, the first line of its brief, with `cast state`, so the inbox and the organization tree show the same headline.
- **Comments** on tasks and plans in its scope, typed progress or decision, so the work items carry the coordination history rather than the lead's transcript.
- **Recommendations and answers** on decisions, through `cast decide`.

It does not post routine updates to chat. A channel full of lead noise trains people to mute it, and the brief is where the update belongs. It posts to chat when asked, when it proposes intake, and when the human's standing brief tells it to, for instance a weekly note in a release channel.

### Replacement

A lead session is replaced for three reasons: it crashed, its context is long, or the human chose "refresh". All three follow the same path. The role's current session pointer moves to a fresh session, which reads the standing brief, the scope notes, the decision log, the latest brief, and the durable task, plan and decision records, and then rewrites the brief. The old session's pending role effects are fenced by generation, as version one specified. No handoff document is needed, and if the old session wrote one it is linked as supplemental context.

The human sees replacement as one line in the brief: "New session from 09:12. Reconstructed from records. Nothing pending was lost." If something was lost, the line says what.

### Choosing a model and a backend

Leads are sessions and inherit the existing backend and model controls. A workspace lead that reads briefs and writes one screen a day is a good fit for a strong model at low volume. A busy plan lead that reviews diffs may want a different point on the cost curve. The hiring flow exposes the same model and effort choice every session has, and the budget contract prices whatever is chosen.
