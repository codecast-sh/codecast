## 1. The thesis

<div class="thesis"><b>In one paragraph</b><p>Codecast today has one manager: you. Every session, whether it is a ten minute fix or a week long migration, settles into the same inbox and waits for the same person to read it, judge it, and decide what happens next. This proposal adds the missing middle. Leads at workspace, project and plan scope each keep a live, sourced account of their scope. They answer the questions they have earned the trust to answer. They direct the work below them within a budget you set. They bring you only the decisions that are yours. The result is not a smaller inbox. It is an organization you can read at any level of detail, from one line about the whole company down to one line of a diff.</p></div>

### The problem, measured on this inbox

The numbers below are a snapshot of the inbox this proposal was written in, taken on 5 September 2026 with `cast sessions --json` and `cast task ls --json`. They are one person's workspace, not a benchmark.

| What | Count |
|---|---:|
| Live sessions in the inbox | 66 |
| Sessions waiting on the human (needs input) | 27 |
| Sessions parked on a machine wake (dormant) | 15 |
| Distinct projects those sessions belong to | 8 |
| Open tasks across the workspace | 522 |
| Tasks in progress right now | 95 |
| Active triggers | about 90 |

27 sessions want the same person's judgment. They span 8 projects. The person has to hold all 8 projects in their head to answer any one of them well. Each card was written by an agent that knows its own task and nothing above it. So the human does the compression: they read the card, remember which project it serves, recall what the plan was, judge whether the question matters, and decide. Then they do it again for the next card, in a different project.

That compression is management. Today it runs on one human. It is the reason the inbox has buckets, states, labels, a decision queue and a settle classifier. All of those help the human triage faster. None of them do the triage. The organization proposal moves the compression into the system, one level at a time, and keeps the human at the top of every ladder.

### What Monday looks like

The experience the proposal is built around is the first 10 minutes of a day.

<ol class="timeline">
<li><time>08:40</time><b>You open the workspace brief.</b><p>One screen written by the workspace lead overnight, sent to you as a direct message with the count of things that need you. What shipped, what is blocked, what changed since you last read. Three decisions that are yours. Cost by project. Every claim links to the task, session, diff or chat message it came from.</p></li>
<li><time>08:42</time><b>You skim what was handled without you.</b><p>Eleven decisions the leads answered under grants you gave them, two stalls they cleared, one dead worker replaced. Each line has the answer, one line of why, and a key to say you would have chosen otherwise. You press it once. That one override lands on the lead's record.</p></li>
<li><time>08:44</time><b>You answer two decisions from the brief.</b><p>Each card shows who asked, which lead recommended what and why. One is a deployment. One is a product choice the plan lead could not settle. The third you leave for later. The queue shows it as yours, not lost.</p></li>
<li><time>08:47</time><b>You zoom into Codecast.</b><p>The project lead's account: three plans moving, one stalled on a flaky test, the stall already assigned to a worker 2 hours ago. You do not open the worker. You could.</p></li>
<li><time>08:49</time><b>You ask the project lead a question in chat.</b><p>"Is the sync work going to land this week?" It answers from its brief and, where the brief is thin, from the live task tree and the workers' state lines. It says what it does not know.</p></li>
<li><time>08:50</time><b>You go and do your own work.</b><p>Your own sessions sit where they always did.</p></li>
</ol>

Everything in that morning already has a primitive in codecast: anchors, tasks, plans, projects, decisions, chat, triggers, state lines, publish. The proposal composes them into an organization. It adds the few things that are missing. They are leads at project and plan scope, a brief that is a first class object, recommendations on the decision ladder, and a way for trust to grow from evidence rather than from settings.

### What version one settled and what this version adds

Version one of this proposal (plan pl-519, published 4 September 2026) was written as a control plane specification. It settled the hard safety questions and the answers stand. In particular: a role is durable and its session is temporary; original spawn provenance never changes; every managed launch is admitted against a budget before it exists; six classes of effect always reach a human; leads read only what their audience may read; a lead can crash and be replaced from durable records without a handoff document. Chapter 11 keeps those contracts and points at the version one chapters that define them.

Version one specified the surfaces as field lists, copy and states, the digest as a data contract, and a first 10 minutes scenario. It did not design the reading experience: the shape of a brief and its reading order, the morning brief, the tree, how trust moves from the human to a lead at the moment of a decision, and the chain between levels. It also chose to start from observation of selected tasks and to defer plan leads to its last gate. This version designs the parts that were missing. Where it changes a version one decision it says so, with the reason, in chapter 12.

<div class="note"><b>Scope of this document</b><p>This is a proposal. It authorizes nothing. It does not deploy a feature, spend money, adopt existing sessions or change anyone's permissions. Examples use real project names from this workspace to make them concrete, and invented events to make them readable. Measured numbers are labeled as measured.</p></div>
