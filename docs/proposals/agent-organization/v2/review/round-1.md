# Review round one, 5 September 2026

Seven critics read the first complete draft in parallel: product, architecture fit, visual design, safety and cost, writing, the agent's side, and consistency with version one. Each finding was to be checked by two adversarial verifiers; the account hit its session limit partway through, so about half the verifications ran and the rest were judged by the author against the code and the text. Findings and dispositions below. The full return is in the workflow journal `wf_a550866c-04d`.

## Design changes made

- **Decisions are a race, not a relay.** Three critics independently found that the draft held ordinary decisions at leads before the human saw them, which is slower than today and contradicts version one. Every decision is now visible in the human's queue at insertion; leads attach recommendations in parallel with a 5 minute hop deadline; a lead with a grant may answer first. Chapters 5, 9, 10, 11, 12 and the ladder figure.
- **The server assigns the class.** The asker's label can only tighten routing; an action request or a conservative classifier decides; options naming a protected effect pin the holder to the human; a lead's answer to free text grants no capability. Chapter 5 and 11.
- **Overrides survive a grant.** A "What was handled" section in every brief lists each granted answer with a one key override; every granted answer is also shown as a card in the first week and one in five after; grants expire after 30 days; two consecutive overrides revoke. Chapters 3, 4, 5, 10, 12 and the brief mockup.
- **Agreement is credited to the originating lead only.** Concurring hops earn nothing; the lead's own asks and advisory defaults do not count; three agreements from two askers unlock the control. Chapter 5, decision and health mockups.
- **Several humans.** Responsible human per scope (owner on projects and plans, assignee on tasks); decisions climb to the asker's responsible human; the brief is one document rendered per reader with per claim audience and per reader coverage; grants belong to the scope. Chapter 4 "For several humans", chapters 3 and 11.
- **Stage one credential.** Today's token identifies a user, not a session, so a stage one lead would hold its host's full authority. A per session role token and a server allow list are now stage one work, before the first dogfood step. Chapters 8, 10, 11.
- **Independent review.** A human sets the review policy; an independent reviewer comes from a different delegation root than the worker's lead; "verified" is reserved for that; a lead's own check is "checked by the lead". Chapters 4, 7, 9, 12 and the glossary.
- **Stalls.** A worker with an open decision, a permission prompt or a limit park is not stalled; stall messages are admitted against budget and capped at two per task per day; dead means daemon confirmed; replacement goes through the fenced task claim. Chapter 7 and the failure table.
- **The lead's hands.** Wake message contract, acknowledgment by the state line, `cast org scope`, `cast org brief write`, `cast decide escalate` and `answer` with version one's verb names, decision short ids (`cd-`), no `cast ws destroy`, no labels for scope, `cast sessions --reports-to`. Chapters 8 and 9.
- **Reuse the code has.** Channel binding through the existing channel to anchor table; wakes through the anchor's delivery function with the client id as the coalescing key; workers nested by pointing the task spawn function's hidden parent at the lead; project updates of the digest kind carry the brief; budgets and grants in version one's policy revisions; decision fields with version one's names and a paged hops table. Chapters 6 and 11.
- **Honest claims.** Mobile has no decision card today; lead sessions are high churn and file stashed and hidden; version one is described by what it did design; the roadmap plan pl-529 has no tasks attached; the two version one rows the change table got wrong were removed and four kept contracts were added.
- **Writing.** No em dashes, no hyphenated compounds, no coined labels, one name per idea, American spelling checked by the validator, digits for thresholds, numbers stated once in chapter 8, long sentences split, restated rules given one home, chapters 4 and 11 opening with their conclusion.
- **Design.** Level colours reserved for level objects; chapter borders neutral; phone layout fixed (legend hidden, SVG and wide tables scroll, mockup panes wrap); contrast tokens darkened; mockups in the app's mono font with level glyph letters; hire flow with fields, host and payer, and a disabled button until a budget is typed; health table with rates and bars; opinion, claimed and changed treatments in the brief; no primary styled option on any decision card; a second confirmation on protected classes.

## Findings not adopted

- Render every chapter in a verb colour (understand, decide, direct). Rejected: three more hues would compete with the level system; chapters are neutral instead.
- Make the brief's delivery an email. Not adopted: the anchor's direct message and mobile push are the existing channels; email is a later choice.
- Keep per task adoption from version one. Rejected with the reason recorded in chapter 12: per task adoption is a permissions page in another form.
