## 2. The model

### Four levels, one human

The organization has four levels of work and one human at the top. Each level already exists in codecast as a work item. The proposal gives three of them a standing agent, and it leaves the fourth to the workers who already do it.

<div class="ladder">
<div class="rung" data-level="workspace"><b>Workspace<small>team or personal</small></b><p><b>Who</b>The workspace lead. This is the existing anchor, the standing agent member a team or a person already has today.</p><p><b>Understands</b>Which projects are moving, which are stalled, what needs you today, what it all costs. Writes the morning brief.</p></div>
<div class="rung" data-level="project"><b>Project</b><p><b>Who</b>A project lead. New. One per project that a human chooses to give one.</p><p><b>Understands</b>The project's plans and loose tasks, its priorities, its risks, its chat channel, what shipped and what is verified. Answers "how is Codecast doing".</p></div>
<div class="rung" data-level="plan"><b>Plan</b><p><b>Who</b>A plan lead. New. Optional. Used when a plan is large enough to need a coordinator of its own.</p><p><b>Understands</b>One bounded outcome: its tasks, their order, their evidence, the decisions taken along the way. Reviews workers' results against the plan's acceptance criteria.</p></div>
<div class="rung" data-level="task"><b>Task</b><p><b>Who</b>A worker. This is any session bound to a task with <code>cast task start</code>, exactly as today. Human sessions and agent sessions alike.</p><p><b>Understands</b>One task: the code, the diff, the tests, the acceptance criteria. Reports up with a state line and evidence.</p></div>
</div>

A lead is a role, not a session. The role is durable. It has a scope, a standing brief, a set of things it is trusted to decide, a budget, and a rhythm. The session that currently holds the role is temporary. It can be replaced, restarted or rotated without the role losing its history or its obligations. Version one established this separation and this version keeps it.

The human is not a level. The human is the top of every ladder and the owner of every scope. A lead never owns a task, a plan or a project. A named person does. A lead coordinates on that person's behalf, within what they have granted.

### Levels can be missing

An organization does not need every level filled. The proposal treats a missing level as the normal case, not an error state.

| Shape | What happens |
|---|---|
| A project with a lead and no plan leads | The project lead coordinates every plan and every loose task in the project directly. This is the expected starting shape. |
| A plan with a lead inside a project with no lead | The plan lead reports to the workspace lead, or to the human if there is none. |
| A project with no lead at all | Nothing changes. Its sessions and tasks behave exactly as today. The workspace brief lists the project from raw task and session state and says so. |
| A workspace with no anchor | Project leads report to the human directly. The morning brief does not exist until the human creates an anchor. |
| A task with no plan and no project | It is loose work. The workspace lead sees it. No lead directs it unless a human files it somewhere. |

The reporting line for any work item is computed from where it is filed, never from who launched it. A task in a plan reports to the plan lead if one exists, else to the project lead of the plan's project, else to the workspace lead, else to the human. Refiling a task moves its reporting line. Who originally spawned the session that works on it never changes and never grants authority.

### What a lead does

Every lead does three things, at its own level, and nothing else.

<div class="compare">
<div><b>Understand</b><p>Keep a live account of its scope, written for the level above. Sourced from task states, session state lines, diffs, decisions, chat and the briefs of the leads below it. Honest about coverage and freshness.</p></div>
<div><b>Decide</b><p>Answer questions from below that fall within what it has been trusted with. Forward the rest upward with a recommendation attached. Never answer a question that belongs to a human.</p></div>
<div><b>Direct</b><p>Start, brief, redirect and review the work below it, within the budget and the allowed effects the human granted. Notice stalls. Assign reviewers. Close what is verified.</p></div>
<div><b>And not</b><p>A lead does not write code, does not own work, does not take over human sessions, does not spend outside its budget, and does not hide anything from the human that the human asked to see.</p></div>
</div>

The three verbs are ordered. A lead that only understands is already useful: that is the first rollout stage. A lead that also decides removes a class of interruption. A lead that also directs removes a class of work. Trust moves down that list one step at a time, per lead, and it moves because the human has seen the lead be right, not because a setting was flipped. Chapter 5 defines that mechanism.

### One organization, not a fleet

Codecast already has ways to run many agents at once: `cast spawn` fan outs, subagent rows nested under a parent, plan orchestration with implementer and reviewer agents, and labels for filing a fleet. Those are batches. They have a start and an end and an orchestrator that goes away.

An organization is different in one way that matters: it persists. A project lead is there on Tuesday because it was there on Monday. It remembers what it decided last week and why. It notices that the same flaky test has stalled three plans. A fan out cannot notice that, because nothing lives long enough to.

The proposal does not replace batches. A lead uses them. When a plan lead needs five tasks done it spawns five workers and files them under itself, exactly as a human orchestrator does today. The organization is the layer that remembers between batches.

<div class="note"><b>Naming</b><p>The proposal uses <em>lead</em> for the role at workspace, project and plan scope, <em>worker</em> for a session bound to a task, and <em>brief</em> for the live account a lead keeps of its scope. The existing anchor becomes the workspace lead. The word anchor stays in the CLI for compatibility. No new vocabulary is needed to describe the whole design.</p></div>
