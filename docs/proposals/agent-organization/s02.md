## 2. The model and a day of work

### A durable lead, temporary sessions, named humans

A lead is a durable role attached to work. A session is the current conversation and process carrying out that role. Workers are temporary sessions assigned to bounded execution. A named human remains responsible for the project or plan. Task ownership, review responsibility, hosting, and billing remain separate facts.

The proposed model generalizes anchors at workspace, project, and plan scope. Tasks keep their existing depth limit and receive workers. Adding a reporting role does not add task levels. A subtask can be executed separately where the task model permits it, while its coordinator follows the same valid scope chain.

| Scope | Coordination responsibility | When no dedicated lead exists |
|---|---|---|
| Workspace, team or personal | Coordinate approved work across projects and surface unresolved responsibilities | Named humans retain direct coordination |
| Project | Maintain project context, sequence adopted work, arrange reviews, and report material changes | An available authorized workspace lead may coordinate adopted work |
| Plan | Coordinate a bounded outcome and its tasks | Its project lead handles adopted work, or the available workspace lead if the plan has no project |
| Task and subtasks | A worker executes agreed criteria; a reviewer checks evidence | The task's human owner determines the next step |

One scope has one accountable lead. Missing levels remain missing. A project can have a lead without a workspace lead. A plan can exist without a project. A task can be filed directly in a project without a plan. The product should show each arrangement without treating absent roles as errors.

### Resolve scope before assigning coordination

Explicit work bindings determine reporting independently of launch provenance. For a task in a plan, the plan's project determines the project scope. A separately stored task project must agree. A subtask must agree with its parent's filing. Workspace disagreement is rejected before a reporting assignment or execution can be created.

| Situation | Proposed resolution | Human consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Task has a plan and matching project | Resolve the plan lead, then project lead, then workspace lead | Show the selected coordinator and the reason |
| Task has a project and no plan | Resolve the project lead, then workspace lead | No plan creation is needed |
| Plan has no project | Resolve the plan lead, then workspace lead | Keep the plan independent |
| Task project differs from its plan's project | Reject the conflicting filing | Offer “Move the plan” or “Remove task from plan” through a scope preview |
| Parent and subtask have conflicting scope | Reject the conflicting filing | Resolve the work structure before starting an attempt |
| Linked objects have different workspaces | Reject the binding | No implicit sharing or management across workspaces |

A scope move is a versioned change. Its preview identifies the current and proposed coordinator, affected tasks, running attempts, access changes, pending decisions, and budget attribution. Confirmation reconciles the affected records against that version. A concurrent move or grant change invalidates the preview. Running work pauses where its authorization no longer applies. New authority requires confirmation before execution resumes.

An existing reporting assignment stays attached to the durable role when its session becomes vacant. The system computes an available escalation target separately. This preserves accountability while letting an urgent question reach someone who can act. A task should never appear to change managers merely because a process crashed.

A role reads only when the workspace gate, resource ACL, and scoped role grant all allow it. Scope visibility grants no execution authority. Retrieval and rendering recheck the source audience.

### Keep responsibility and infrastructure separate

The lead's durable identity survives replacement of its current session. `anchor.conversation_id` and a monotonic generation identify the current holder. `reports_to_anchor_id` remains stable. The displayed parent resolves through the current role session map. Original `spawned_by_conversation_id` and fork lineage remain unchanged forever. Existing containment and Claude team nesting keep their meanings.

| Fact | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Responsible human | Accountable for the work and for identifying eligible approvers |
| Task owner | Owns the task's agreed outcome; adoption does not silently reassign it |
| Lead identity | Coordinates a defined scope under its current grant |
| Current session and generation | Holds the role now; old generations cannot continue role effects |
| Working session | Holds a specific execution attempt, with its own evidence and history |
| Reviewer | Checks the result against criteria and the reviewed revision |
| Host and device | Supply the runtime and resources |
| Billing account | Pays for approved compute under explicit configuration |

Bot users stay out of `session_owners`. Human ownership of a session remains human ownership even when the conversation is linked to adopted work. Hosting a lead does not make the host the authorized approver. If no eligible person is available, the product says “Waiting for an authorized owner. No approval has been granted.”

Authentication must establish the actor and its scope. A supplied session ID or `--from` value is attribution only. Section 5 defines the required control boundary. The product must not present a worker using shared human credentials as independently constrained authority.

### Observation and managed automation

Adoption records `automation_mode`: `manual`, `observed`, or `managed`. Manual work follows its existing human control. Observed work allows the lead to inspect approved evidence and recommend a next step. Managed work permits defined actions under a grant, supported runtime, and explicit human consent. Neither bot identity nor spawn provenance changes that mode.

Observation of a human session is itself explicit and scoped. It does not permit the lead to message, stop, hide, or take over the session. A managed task can also have human conversations linked as evidence. Those links do not make the conversations managed workers.

Workers may choose reversible implementation details inside approved acceptance criteria. Leads handle coordination and unresolved ambiguity within their grant. Purchases or billing changes, destructive data changes, production changes, auth or access changes, external communication, and unresolved user facing product choices require a human decision. Unknown effect classes escalate. Drafting can continue only where it is independently authorized and does not commit the protected choice.

Runtime state and next actor answer different questions. A process can be alive while a human owes approval. A stopped lead can have a recovery action assigned to another authorized lead. “Waiting” therefore names both the reason and the actor who can change it. Section 4 describes delivery and escalation in detail.

### One accountable lead with scoped experts

A project lead may request expertise from another project without sharing management authority. The request names the accountable lead, question, allowed artifacts, deliverable, desired response time, and approved cost allocation. The expert receives only the permitted inputs and returns evidence to the requesting task. It cannot reassign the task, inherit workspace access, or direct the requesting workers.

An illustrative consultation reads: “Review this proposed token validation change. Share the patch and test output only. Return security concerns and suggested checks. Mail lead remains accountable.” The expert's response is advisory evidence. If the lead implemented the change, an eligible independent reviewer still has to verify the final revision where review policy requires it.

Project and plan leads also have distinct ownership. A plan lead coordinates its bounded result. The project lead coordinates dependencies and competing priorities across adopted plans. A conflict about scope or acceptance reaches the named human. Two leads cannot issue competing directions to the same active task attempt.

### A full day, illustrative

The following day is illustrative. Names, times, task counts, and events are invented. It assumes the managed project gate has passed. Mail has one project lead, a responsible human named Maya, and selected tasks with explicit consent. It has no workspace or plan lead. Another project supplies an expert consultation under a separate request.

| Time | Event and evidence | Next actor |
|---|---|---|
| 08:30 | Maya opens Mail. The brief links a verified replay test, an unresolved fixture blocker, and a proposed notification fix. It marks production behavior unverified. | Maya selects the proposal to inspect |
| 09:00 | Maya confirms the exact scope, acceptance criteria, runtime, payer, and configured allowance. A task attempt and execution permit are admitted once. | The admitted worker starts reproduction |
| 10:10 | The worker reports a missing fixture. The lead finds an accessible earlier task with the correct fixture path and records the source in its reply. | The worker resumes within the approved attempt |
| 11:15 | The patch raises a token validation concern. The lead requests a bounded review from the shared expert, with only the patch and test output shared. | The expert returns findings; the Mail lead retains coordination |
| 12:30 | A proposed retry cannot fit the remaining reservation and ancestor limits. The lead presents existing evidence and the reason admission was declined. | Maya may approve a revised compute allocation or leave the attempt paused |
| 13:20 | A human asks in the project channel whether customer notifications can be changed. The lead previews a separate intake proposal and its audience. | The authorized human edits or confirms the proposal |
| 14:40 | The worker reports completion. Its evidence contains the patch revision, replay command, result, and known limitations. | The assigned reviewer checks that revision |
| 15:30 | Review verifies the agreed local behavior. A deployment request reaches Maya directly as a protected decision. Lead advice appears when ready. | Maya decides on the exact deployment request |
| 17:00 | Maya reads the update: accepted local result, deployment still pending, one review concern resolved, and the remaining admission limit. | Maya owns the deployment decision; the lead has no new execution to start |

The budget event does not imply permission to buy a service. A compute allowance funds only the supported model activity it names. Human answers remain recordable even if no compute remains to process them. The interface distinguishes “Answer saved” from “Worker resumed.”

The final update does not say the project is on track unless a target and evidence support that claim. It separates reported completion, verification against criteria, and owner acceptance. Task completion does not approve a release. No role is added because the day became busy. Archive only verified clean work after the chosen retention period. Dirty worktrees, unmerged commits, pending approvals, and unresolved effects remain protected. Time alone does not authorize deletion.

### A failed manager, illustrative

This failure scenario is illustrative. The Mail lead reports that a fix is verified, but its evidence points to an older revision. Maya opens the claim, follows the source link, and sees the mismatch. She chooses “Take over review.” The current acceptance claim becomes disputed. New dependent dispatch pauses while the exact affected work is identified.

The lead then crashes before writing a handoff. Its role remains present and vacant. The durable inbox retains unacknowledged events. Task records retain active attempts, pending decisions, and evidence. Replacement reconstructs approved scope and unresolved obligations from those records. A handoff document can assist, but recovery does not depend on the failed session producing one.

Refill advances the generation with a generation compare and swap check and revokes the old capability. If the old session resumes, every attempted role effect is fenced. Duplicate event delivery may occur; the operation identifier prevents a second effect. An external action with an uncertain result enters reconciliation before any retry.

Maya is the next actor for the disputed acceptance. The replacement lead is the next actor for reconstructing the review packet. The worker may continue only independent, already authorized work. Stopping the lead session vacates the role; stopping a managed subtree is a separate previewed action. Completed effects may require repair. Neither override nor refill silently undoes them.
