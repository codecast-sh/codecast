Fix a gap in codecast: role wakes carry no sender.

`cast role wake @handle "<text>"` delivers into the role's standing session as a plain, unattributed user turn. `cast send <session> "<text>"` delivers the same kind of text wrapped as `<session-message from="<sender short id>">…</session-message>`, so the recipient knows who wrote it and where a reply goes. A role wake has no sender, so replies never route back.

## What happened (Oct 1, Union workspace)
The Chief of Staff session (jx79zjy) woke six leads (@growth, @agent-quality, @calling, @infra, @private-network, @deals) with "…send me only what needs Ashot." All six did the work, but each read "me" as its own human. They answered in their own threads and raised decision cards to Ashot, and nothing came back to the Chief of Staff. The Private Network lead (jx78kyq) also treated the wake as untrusted pasted text and acted cautiously on it. Evidence: `cast search "Your area reads stuck" -s 3d` shows the wakes landing as bare `[user]` turns, e.g. jx7d538 line 1 and jx7f4ez line 1.

## Goal
1. A role wake sent from inside a session is delivered exactly like `cast send` from that session: same `<session-message from="<short id>">` wrapper and the same sender resolution code, reused, not reimplemented. The role's standing session can reply with `cast send <sender>` and the reply lands in the sender's thread.
2. A role wake sent by a human from a terminal (no session) is attributed to that person (the existing `name=` form), not left anonymous.
3. Wakes the system fires on its own schedule (a role's daily check, area-change alerts) keep their current framing; only person or session wakes change.
4. The role's instructions (charter preamble or wake framing) say plainly that a wake from a session is a colleague's message, and that when it asks for a result, the reply goes to that session with `cast send`. Write it at principle level, with no scripted phrasing.
